# ADR-0006: Governed audit/outbox envelope and durable persistence

- **Status:** Accepted for the stacked implementation branch; not protected-main truth until merged.
- **Decision date:** 2026-08-17
- **Scope:** Orgmetra-owned audit envelope, immutable audit persistence, guarded outbox delivery state, tenant-safe atomic dispatcher claiming, expired-lease takeover, live-owner completion/retry, and database-budget-governed terminal dead-letter escalation. Exponential retry policy, retention/export workflows, and external delivery receipts remain subsequent work.

## Context

Orgmetra mutations need portable event envelopes for asynchronous integration and durable, attributable evidence for later review. Copying mutable HR payloads into audit/event records would create a shadow system of record and enlarge the PII retention surface. Omitting actor, purpose, reason, evidence version, or accountable human confirmation would make high-impact employment changes difficult to explain and govern.

CloudEvents 1.0 provides a stable interoperable event envelope. NIST SP 800-92 requires protected, accountable log-management evidence. PostgreSQL row-level security and trigger semantics provide an Orgmetra-owned enforcement boundary for tenant isolation, append-only audit facts, and constrained delivery-state transitions. PostgreSQL documents `SKIP LOCKED` as suitable for avoiding lock contention among multiple consumers of queue-like tables; Orgmetra uses that primitive only for the inconsistent queue view where skipping work already locked by another dispatcher is the intended behavior.

A lease-based dispatcher also needs a crash boundary. Treating `leased` as permanently ineligible would strand a delivery forever when the owning worker dies after claiming it. Recovery therefore has to distinguish a live lease from an expired ownership grant, without deleting or rewriting the immutable audit fact. The lease must also be an executable capability boundary: a worker that merely knows a delivery identifier must not be able to acknowledge, release, or terminally discard another worker's live lease, and a worker whose lease expired must not finalize after ownership can legitimately move elsewhere.

Retries also need a terminal failure boundary. Without an explicit retry budget, a permanently rejected downstream contract can cycle forever; if a dispatcher can choose that budget at finalization time, a compromised or defective worker can instead discard first-attempt work. The retry budget therefore belongs to durable delivery state and is immutable once the delivery exists. Without immutable escalation evidence, an operator could also make a serious integration failure disappear by mutating queue state. The terminal decision therefore has to be tied to a still-live lease, the stored retry budget, a bounded failure classification, and an append-only external escalation reference.

## Decision

`orgmetra_hris_kernel.AuditOutboxEvent` is the canonical in-process envelope builder for HRIS mutations. `database/migrations/0003_audit_outbox_persistence.sql` is the durable persistence boundary for that envelope, `database/migrations/0004_outbox_delivery_claim.sql` adds dispatcher claim and expired-lease recovery, `database/migrations/0005_outbox_delivery_finalization.sql` adds owner-bound completion and retry, and `database/migrations/0006_outbox_delivery_dead_letter.sql` adds a durable delivery retry budget, terminal dead-lettering, and normalized immutable escalation evidence without changing immutable audit bytes.

The contract:

1. Emits CloudEvents `specversion: 1.0`, stable event id, `urn:orgmetra:<service>` source, `orgmetra.*` event type, opaque subject reference, UTC event time, and JSON content type.
2. Carries tenant, actor, purpose, reason, evidence-version, and optional human-confirmation references as Orgmetra extension attributes.
3. Requires a confirmation reference for a high-impact event rather than manufacturing or inferring human review.
4. Carries only mutation result classification in `data`; mutable names, compensation, free-text evidence, and other HR payload fields stay in their authoritative bounded context.
5. Exposes `canonical_json()` as the exact deterministic UTF-8 JSON representation to persist and digest; `content_digest()` is SHA-256 over those exact bytes.
6. Stores immutable event evidence in `audit_event_record`. PostgreSQL reparses the envelope, requires the exact allowlisted field shape, verifies event/tenant binding and high-impact confirmation, and recomputes SHA-256 over the supplied canonical bytes before accepting the row.
7. Stores asynchronous delivery lifecycle separately in `outbox_delivery_record`, preserving 3NF and preventing dispatcher state from rewriting immutable audit evidence. Terminal dead-letter escalation metadata is normalized again into one `outbox_delivery_escalation_record` per terminally failed delivery rather than copied into the audit fact.
8. Permits guarded `pending -> leased -> delivered` delivery transitions, `leased -> pending` retry recovery, only after lease expiry an atomic `leased -> leased` ownership takeover, and `leased -> dead_lettered` only after the durable retry budget is exhausted and matching immutable escalation evidence exists. Delivery identity, audit binding, and retry budget never change; delivered and dead-lettered records are terminal. Every new ownership grant must expire strictly after the transaction timestamp.
9. `record_audit_outbox_event(...)` inserts the immutable audit fact and pending delivery record in one PostgreSQL statement. The owning service calls it inside the same database transaction as its business mutation so any later statement failure rolls the whole transaction back. Migration 0006 gives every delivery an immutable `maximum_attempt_count` of 5 by default, constrained to 1 through 100 for future producer/policy configuration; dispatchers do not supply or lower it while finalizing a failure.
10. Forces tenant row-level security on audit, delivery, and escalation relations through the existing `current_tenant_record_id()` contract.
11. `claim_outbox_delivery(...)` requires its requested tenant to equal the active tenant context, validates delivery-target and opaque worker identifiers, bounds a lease to 1–3600 seconds, and claims at most one due pending or expired leased delivery in deterministic order.
12. The claim reads candidates with `FOR UPDATE ... SKIP LOCKED`, updates the chosen row through the transition guard, increments the attempt count once, sets a future lease, and returns the immutable canonical event plus digest needed by the dispatcher. A live lease is skipped rather than stolen or duplicated.
13. When the selected row carries an expired lease, takeover increments the attempt count again and writes `last_failure_code = 'lease_expired'`. This makes worker loss observable while preserving the original event, target, recorded time, delivery identity, audit binding, and stored retry budget. A partial index over leased expiry coordinates bounds recovery lookup cost.
14. `complete_outbox_delivery(...)` row-locks the tenant-scoped delivery and requires the exact stored lease owner plus a lease expiry strictly after the transaction timestamp before setting terminal delivery state. A foreign or stale worker fails closed.
15. `retry_outbox_delivery(...)` enforces the same live-owner requirement, validates a lower `snake_case` failure code, bounds retry delay to 1–86400 seconds, preserves the attempt count, clears lease metadata, and schedules the row back to pending no earlier than the bounded retry time. Expired work must be reclaimed through `claim_outbox_delivery(...)` before it can be completed or retried.
16. `dead_letter_outbox_delivery(...)` enforces the same tenant/live-owner capability boundary and validates an operational escalation id, lower `snake_case` failure code, and namespaced opaque escalation reference. It reads the immutable `maximum_attempt_count` from the locked delivery row and rejects terminal failure until `delivery_attempt_count >= maximum_attempt_count`; there is no dispatcher-supplied attempt-budget parameter.
17. A successful dead-letter operation runs under the locked delivery row, inserts exactly one tenant-scoped `outbox_delivery_escalation_record` with the terminal attempt count/failure/reference, then transitions the delivery to `dead_lettered`, clears lease metadata, and preserves terminal failure evidence. The transition guard itself requires exhausted stored budget and matching escalation evidence, so direct table DML cannot skip the invariant. A deferred binding constraint rejects an escalation record unless the referenced delivery commits as matching terminal `dead_lettered` state. The escalation relation is append-only under forced tenant RLS; normal claiming never selects `dead_lettered` rows.

## Consequences

- A buyer can trace an HRIS mutation to tenant, actor, purpose, reason, evidence version, and human confirmation without duplicating the underlying HR record.
- Event consumers receive a CloudEvents-compatible envelope while service extraction remains possible.
- A digest mismatch, extra top-level payload field, tenant/event-id mismatch, or missing high-impact confirmation fails closed at the durable database boundary rather than relying only on application validation.
- Immutable audit facts and mutable delivery coordination remain distinct relations; retries and leases cannot rewrite historical evidence.
- Multiple dispatcher workers have a bounded, tenant-scoped claim primitive that skips rows already locked by another consumer, refuses live-lease theft, and can recover work after an ownership lease actually expires.
- Crash recovery no longer requires rewriting a leased row back to pending first. The same locked claim operation can take over an expired lease, preserving one-row ownership while recording the failed attempt.
- Delivery acknowledgment, retry, and terminal failure are tied to a still-live lease capability. A worker cannot complete, release, or dead-letter another worker's row, and an expired owner cannot race a legitimate replacement owner.
- Permanent downstream failures can leave the normal dispatch queue only after the immutable delivery retry budget is exhausted. The dispatcher cannot lower the budget during finalization, and terminal failure leaves immutable tenant-scoped escalation evidence with a customer/operator reference.
- Dead-lettering does not imply downstream acknowledgement. Exponential/backoff policy selection, external delivery receipts, and crash/restart evidence remain required before audit delivery is called production-ready.
- Retention, customer export, backup/restore validation, and privileged audit access remain explicit operational controls rather than properties inferred from the digest alone.
- Downstream services dereference authoritative records through published owner contracts rather than expecting copied PII in event payloads.

## Evidence

The stacked branch contains test-first regression coverage for CloudEvents shape, PII minimization, deterministic canonical bytes/digest behavior, high-impact confirmation, timezone ambiguity, source/event namespace validation, runtime type confusion, and identifier validity. The PostgreSQL contracts additionally exercise atomic audit/outbox insertion, database digest verification, PII-bearing extra-field rejection, missing-confirmation rejection, append-only audit protection, guarded lease/delivery transitions, terminal-state immutability, rollback on outbox failure, rejection of already-expired new leases, deterministic due-work claiming, live-lease exclusion, tenant-context binding, opaque worker identity, bounded lease duration, takeover of a lease that expires after a successful claim with a second attempt plus `lease_expired` evidence, foreign-owner completion/retry rejection, successful live-owner completion, successful bounded live-owner retry, stale-owner completion rejection after expiry, rejection of a dispatcher-controlled attempt-budget signature, rejection of direct premature terminal DML, rejection of premature dead-lettering against the stored budget, foreign-owner dead-letter denial, successful dead-letter at the default fifth attempt, non-reclaimability of dead-lettered rows, rejection of fabricated nonterminal escalation evidence, and append-only escalation evidence.

The original dead-letter regression was committed before the owning migration existed, so its first execution path was structurally RED at `dead_letter_outbox_delivery(...)`. A second RED strengthened the contract after review found that the first draft accepted a caller-supplied maximum-attempt argument and allowed structurally valid direct dead-letter DML. Migration 0006 now owns the retry budget and terminal evidence binding; Foundation CI includes the exact PostgreSQL contract. The current execution environment has no PostgreSQL client/server and the branch remains stacked on a non-default base; database execution and the complete Foundation CI/central workflow set therefore remain non-passing until exact-head hosted evidence completes. No queued, absent, predecessor, or model-only result is treated as GREEN.
