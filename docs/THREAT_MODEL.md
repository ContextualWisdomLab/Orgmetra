# Threat Model

## STRIDE summary

| Threat | Example | Preventive control | Detection evidence and required test |
|---|---|---|---|
| Spoofing | External identity treated as HR person identity | Separate `person_record` from the Keyverse subject; verify issuer, audience, tenant, actor binding, and token lifetime. Candidate conversion also binds the resulting person to an existing tenant-local employment rather than trusting a caller-supplied worker claim. | Authentication-denial audit; tests reject subject/person substitution, stale authorization, and employment/person mismatch. |
| Tampering | Selection evidence, candidate conversion lineage, audit envelope, terminal escalation evidence, or delivery history changed after decision | Append-only decision/evidence records; database-sealed evidence digest; bitemporal candidate conversion with immutable-history correction guard; append-only `audit_event_record` and `outbox_delivery_escalation_record`; immutable database-owned delivery retry budget; database recomputation of SHA-256 over exact canonical audit bytes; guarded delivery state stored separately. | Integrity alert; tests reject legacy direct candidate-worker writes, non-hire conversion, conversion overlap/in-place rewrite, evidence update/delete, digest mismatch, added audit fields, illegal outbox state changes, retry-budget mutation/dispatcher override, attempt N+1, terminal-row reopening, escalation mutation/fabrication, and version mismatch. |
| Repudiation | Hiring manager denies a worker conversion or integration operator denies a terminal delivery failure | Candidate conversion references the same-candidate human-confirmed hire decision, actor, tenant, purpose, reason, sealed evidence version, and resulting employment; immutable audit event, recorded dispatcher identity, and immutable terminal escalation reference preserve later transport accountability. | Correlated conversion/decision/evidence/audit/escalation lookup; tests prove actor/confirmation/evidence traceability, prohibit high-impact conversion from a non-hire decision, prohibit high-impact audit persistence without confirmation, and preserve one append-only escalation record for dead-lettered work. |
| Information disclosure | PII broadcast or retained through an event bus, conversion shadow record, or failure queue | Candidate conversion stores opaque identifiers and decision/evidence links rather than names or assessment payloads; exact durable audit-field allowlist, field-level APIs, purpose-bound authorization, tenant-scoped encryption, and no copied mutable HR payload in audit or escalation evidence. | Payload scanner and database contract; tests reject extra employee-name/PII event fields even when their digest is recomputed and constrain conversion/audit/escalation persistence to authoritative identifiers and governance references. |
| Cross-tenant access | Tenant A reads, reconstructs, changes, converts, or emits evidence for Tenant B HRIS facts by altering a path, header, reference, cache key, event, delivery, escalation, or by supplying a foreign fact with a colliding durable identifier to an in-memory decision. | Authenticated tenant context, tenant-qualified candidate/person/employment/decision foreign keys, forced RLS on candidate conversion and audit/outbox/escalation relations, explicit tenant scope in historical reconstruction and HRIS decision functions, audit event/tenant identity binding, scoped authorization, service-owned database roles, tenant-aware cache keys, and consumer-side event validation. | `cross_tenant_access_denied` audit event with no sensitive values; integration/kernel/database tests attempt direct reads, writes, conversion-reference swaps, colliding identifiers, event tenant mismatch, foreign delivery finalization/escalation, cache poisoning, and replay across tenants and require denial or exclusion with unchanged target data. |
| Denial of service | Integration retries flood services, a permanently rejected event loops forever, or final-attempt ownership is lost | Idempotency, guarded outbox leasing, immutable bounded database-owned retry-attempt budget with terminal dead-letter removal, no claim/retry after exhaustion, stable final-attempt worker identity, bounded exponential backoff and queue limits before production dispatcher release, circuit breaking, and per-tenant budgets. | Queue-depth/lease/retry/dead-letter telemetry; load and recovery tests must prove bounded work, pre-exhaustion expired-lease recovery, stored retry-budget exhaustion, no attempt N+1, exhausted-final-lease non-reclaimability, recorded-owner terminalization, and fair tenant isolation before dispatcher release. |
| Elevation of privilege | LLM, integration, or foreign dispatcher grants itself access, converts a candidate, or finalizes another worker's high-impact/transport state | LLM outputs remain draft evidence; policy engine and human confirmation control HR writes; candidate conversion requires an existing sealed human-confirmed `hire` decision for the same candidate and employment/person consistency; database revalidates high-impact confirmation; dispatcher completion/retry require exact live lease ownership; dead-lettering requires exhausted durable budget plus the exact recorded final-attempt worker identity, and never accepts a dispatcher-selected retry threshold. | Authorization-denial audit; tests reject LLM decision records, direct legacy candidate-worker writes, non-hire worker conversion, missing-confirmation audit events, `Offered`/`Worker` transitions without governed lineage, foreign/stale completion or retry, dispatcher retry-budget override, direct premature terminal DML, replacement-worker claim after exhaustion, foreign dead-lettering, fabricated nonterminal escalation evidence, and premature dead-lettering. |

## Model risk

LLM analysis may summarize, extract, or draft but cannot publish job profiles, approve selection, create candidate-to-worker conversion lineage, alter compensation, revise performance policy, or change employment state without a human decision record.

## Data risk

Blanket masking can break HR work. Orgmetra uses purpose-bound access, encryption, retention, audit, and export control so authorized users can work with required PII safely. Candidate conversion stores opaque candidate/person/employment/decision identities plus time coordinates while the exact hiring rationale and evidence remain in their normalized authoritative decision relations. Event and telemetry surfaces minimize PII even when the authoritative service is permitted to display it. `audit_event_record` keeps only the allowlisted governance envelope; delivery retries, leases, immutable retry budget, and terminal state live in `outbox_delivery_record`; immutable dead-letter escalation metadata lives in `outbox_delivery_escalation_record`. This separation prevents mutable transport coordination or terminal operator evidence from rewriting or expanding retained audit evidence. Exhausted final-attempt ownership remains bound to the recorded stable worker reference; transferring that authority requires a future explicit audited operator-recovery contract rather than direct row mutation.
